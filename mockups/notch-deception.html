Notch deception games: mockups

Six ways to take what makes Among Us work (hidden roles, faking normal behaviour, partial views, a group vote) and fit it to the notch. Every island drops from a 185 × 32 pt notch. Islands are larger than Notch Dino's because size is no longer a constraint. Each variant has its own colour so you can tell them apart; the art is placeholder.

01 Outsider

In-room party game, 3–10 people, about 5 minutes. Each notch deals a private card: everyone gets the same secret place, except one outsider, who gets nothing. People take turns asking each other questions out loud. The outsider bluffs and tries to work out the place. The notch only deals cards, keeps time and counts votes. People in the room do the talking.

private cardtalking out loudpermission to lielow build cost
Review
  • Is "glance, then Esc" enough to keep a card private from the person sitting next to you?
  • Should the round screen show question counts, or is that too much pressure?
  • Does the outsider need a clue (category only), or is nothing more fun?

02 Whisper

Office game that runs all day, 4+ people on the same network. A message travels notch to notch through a hidden chain. Each person sees only what they received, rewords it slightly and passes it on in under 30 seconds. One secret corrupter twists it on purpose. At 4pm the whole chain is revealed and everyone votes on where it broke.

partial viewlong quiet, sudden spike10 s per turnopt-in only
Review
  • Is a glow on the folded notch quiet enough, or should whispers wait until you open it yourself?
  • Should the rule be "must change at least one word" so honest drift exists to hide the corrupter?
  • Is 4pm a good reveal time, or should the reveal happen when the chain finishes?

03 Blend

2–6 players, about 2 minutes. An arena full of drifting dots, almost all moved by AI. Each hider secretly steers one dot and must touch three spots in order while moving like a bot. The seeker has three shots to find the humans. A reverse Turing test: the human has to pass as the machine.

faking normal behaviourwatch vs workLAN, 2+ playersAI movement is the hard part
Review
  • Are 3 shots for 1–2 hiders about right, or too generous?
  • Should bots also visit task spots now and then, so touching one isn't proof?
  • Is the trail replay at the end the payoff, or does it need more?

04 Fuse

3–8 players on a LAN, about 3 minutes. Everyone keeps a shared fuse burning by solving quick puzzles. Each solve adds fuel. You can only see what your two neighbours add. One saboteur's solves secretly take fuel away. The fuse reaching the bomb, or a correct vote, ends the round.

watch vs workpartial viewteamwork with a traitor
Review
  • Is "see only neighbours" clear enough, or does it need a seating diagram?
  • Should the saboteur get an obvious tell (slower solves) or none at all?
  • Does the puzzle need to be harder so watching costs more?

05 Daily Liar

Solo, about 2 minutes, one puzzle a day. Five people each give a statement about the same small event. Exactly one is lying. Find the contradiction. Results are shared as a small coloured grid, like Wordle.

deductiondaily habitno networklowest build cost
Review
  • Are five statements readable in the island at this size?
  • Should wrong guesses cost something (score), or just be counted?
  • Hand-written puzzles or generated? Generated needs a logic engine.

06 Alibi

Experimental office game across a whole day, 4+ people. Uses rough real-world presence: Bluetooth signal strength between Macs and whether a lid is open. The hidden "ghost" can only tag someone when their Macs are close and no other Mac is nearby. Your real location is your alibi.

most like Among Usprivacy-heavyvery high build costexperiment only
Review
  • Is even rough proximity too creepy for an office? It needs to be strictly opt-in, auto-off after the game, and shown clearly.
  • Laptops mostly stay on desks. Does the game still work if people rarely move?
  • Would rooms people mark themselves ("I'm in the kitchen") be enough instead of real signals?

Round 2

Eight more variants: games hidden inside your real workday, short in-room rounds, and games with AI in a role.

07 Codeword

Office game across a workday, 4+ people. Each morning the notch privately gives you an odd word or phrase. Slip it into real Slack messages or meetings without anyone noticing. Anyone can flag a suspected smuggle during the day. At 5pm: who smuggled what? Your real workday is the game board; the notch only deals, holds the secret and keeps score.

faking normal behaviourquiet day, 5pm spikealmost no screen timelow build cost
Review
  • Self-reported uses ("I used it") rely on honesty. Good enough among friends?
  • Should flags be limited (3 a day) so people can't flag everything?
  • Should words get harder through the week (Monday "pineapple", Friday "as per my last email")?

08 Target

Office game over several days, 5+ people. The notch gives you a secret target (a coworker) and a task, like getting them to say a word or hand you an object. Succeed, the target confirms in their notch, they're out, and you inherit their target. Last one in wins.

hidden rolesno one to trustruns for dayslow build cost
Review
  • The target confirms their own exit. Is that fair, or should a dispute go to a vote?
  • Are task types (say a word, hand an object, sit in a chair) varied enough?
  • Is a public feed of exits fun or too much pressure?

09 Sneaky Commit

For developers, 3–6 people, about 5 minutes. Everyone gets the same short function and makes one small "refactor" each. One saboteur also hides a real bug inside theirs. Everyone reviews all the diffs and votes on which commit broke it. Tests run at the reveal and prove who it was.

partial viewgroup votemade for developersmedium build cost
Review
  • Should honest players be allowed to make risky-looking but correct changes, to hide the saboteur?
  • Language-agnostic pseudocode, or let the host pick JS/Swift/Python?
  • Is 60 seconds of review per round enough?

10 Different Question

In-room party game, 3–10 people, about 90 seconds. Everyone privately answers a number question. One person secretly got a different question. Answers are revealed together; whoever looks odd has to defend their number out loud, then everyone votes.

blending inaccuse and defend90 s roundslowest build cost
Review
  • Could this be a mode inside Outsider instead of its own game?
  • Should the odd one know they're odd? (Here they don't, which makes it funnier.)
  • Number answers only, or also short words?

11 Fake Sketch

In-room party game, 4–8 people, about 3 minutes. A shared tiny canvas lives in the notch. Each player adds one stroke towards a secret word using the trackpad. One player doesn't know the word and has to add a stroke that looks like they do. Two laps around the table, then vote.

faking normal behaviourtrackpad drawingfunniest revealmedium build cost
Review
  • Is a canvas this size enough, or should the island grow to 700 pt wide for this game?
  • Should the faker get a category hint ("places")?
  • Should the faker be allowed one guess at the word to steal the win?

12 Off Beat

3–8 players on a LAN, about 2 minutes. Everyone taps Space to a shared rhythm to keep the group's streak alive. One saboteur taps slightly off on purpose to break it. The game shows the combined beat and vague hints about who missed. Vote between songs.

teamwork with a traitorone keyreal-timeneeds low lag
Review
  • Honest players miss too. Is that enough cover for the saboteur, or too much?
  • Hints by seat ("someone on the left") or by timing ("someone was early")?
  • Needs sound to work well. Notch Dino has none. OK to add for this?

13 Spot the Bot

3–6 players, about 3 minutes. An anonymous group chat in the notch, everyone under a random nickname. One participant is Claude, told to act like a coworker. Chat, then vote on who's the bot. Flip mode: one human pretends to be the bot and everyone else has to find them.

pure deceptionanonymous chatneeds internetmedium build cost
Review
  • Needs internet and an AI model. That breaks Notch Dino's "no internet" promise, so it would be a separate app or a clearly opt-in mode. OK?
  • Should the bot be told to make typos, or is a too-polished bot the fun tell?
  • Flip mode (human pretends to be the bot) or normal mode first?

14 Sleeper

Solo, about 10 minutes split over 5 short "days". You're the saboteur and the crew are AI. Crew members do tasks, notice where you were and report it at the evening meeting. Sabotage the base without getting voted out. The traitor role, usually the most fun, playable alone.

play the traitorsoloAI that suspects youhigh build cost
Review
  • Rule-based AI crew (cheaper, offline) or a language model (richer, needs internet)?
  • Should days be playable one at a time, like a daily puzzle?
  • Is a suspicion meter per crew member too much of a hint, or a fair one?

Compare

Strongest picks across both rounds: Codeword (most original fit for the notch, tiny build), Outsider with Different Question as a second mode (cheapest party games, the room does the talking), and Sleeper if you want a solo game. Blend remains the most ambitious design.
